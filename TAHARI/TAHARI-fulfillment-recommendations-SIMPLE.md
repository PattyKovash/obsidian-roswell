## **Problem 1: International orders pulling from stores** {#problem-1:-international-orders-pulling-from-stores}

Stores cannot ship internationally at a sensible cost, so these orders get cancelled or shipped at a loss.

The request is to allow only the CLS warehouse to fulfill international orders.

### **How your orders are routed today** {#how-your-orders-are-routed-today}

Shopify runs a checklist on every order. Each step rules locations out, and whoever is left gets the order.

1. **"Can one location send this in one box?"**  
2. **"Prefer CLS, then the three stores"**

**The problem is the order.** Step 1 runs first and knows nothing about CLS.

An order of 3 items. CLS has 2 of them, Santa Monica has all 3\. Step 1 picks Santa Monica because it can send one box. CLS never gets considered.

This happens whenever CLS is missing an item and another location, or combination of locations, can complete the order in fewer packages.

### **Our recommendation** {#our-recommendation}

**Add a checkout Fulfillment Constraint function so international orders can only be fulfilled by CLS.**

- At checkout, after users enter their shipping address, if an item cannot be fulfilled by CLS, the user is shown a message and can have the item removed from checkout.  
    
- Nothing changes for US orders. No effect on your shipping rates.

**Considerations**

- The customer finds out partway through checkout rather than earlier at the time of adding to cart. Shopify shows them which item is the problem and offers to remove it at checkout after the shipping address is entered.  
    
  > **IMPORTANT.** Please check with your legal team whether a disclaimer would be required up front that some items cannot ship internationally.  
    
- We could add earlier user feedback on the website with additional work and custom logic. **We suggest not doing this yet.** International is 0.3% of orders. It is worth seeing whether the checkout message plus a legal disclaimer, if required, is good enough first.  
    
  **The additional work would involve:**  
    
  - Writing the warehouse stock figure to each product behind the scenes  
  - Showing the item as unavailable to international shoppers, on product pages and product cards, tested across all markets  
  - A second checkout rule to power the message in the bag  
  - Wiring that message into the theme


  This also makes the website say something Shopify does not agree with. See [**When implementing inventory workarounds that do not use native Shopify availability**](#when-implementing-inventory-workarounds-that-do-not-use-native-shopify-availability:) at the end.


- Other store locations cannot fulfill an international order, even when they are the only location with the item in stock.

### **Alternate options considered** {#alternate-options-considered}

The below options were also investigated. Options 1 and 2 were configured and tested on a test store against real checkout scenarios, option 4 was tested to confirm how it behaves.

**1\. Split the locations into two groups in your shipping settings**

CLS goes in one group that ships worldwide. The three stores go in a group that ships to the US only. No development, and we tested it working.

**Why not:**

- Split US orders would be charged two shipping rates added together. A $200 order goes from about $15 to $30  
- Your US shipping rates would have to exist in two places and match exactly, forever  
- Unknown effect on any rates generated by an app. Today everything quotes from New Jersey. This would also ask them to quote from Florida, California and Maryland, and if they are not set up for that, some **US** orders could get no shipping options at all  
- Every downside lands on US orders to fix an international problem. The US accounts for 99.4% of your orders against 0.3% international, so this asks your dominant market to carry the risk for a small one

**2\. Reorder the routing rules so Location Priority is before Minimize Split Shipment**

No development. Swaps the two rules described at the top.

**Why not:**

- It only makes CLS *preferred*, not required. If CLS is out of an item, a store still fulfils the international order  
- More US orders would ship in two packages, and we absorb the second one  
- Still worth considering separately if you want CLS used more for US orders, but it does not solve this problem

**3\. A separate shipping profile for international**

**Why not:**

- A product can only sit in one shipping profile at a time, so moving products into an international profile removes them from your main one  
- It would mean duplicate products and keeping their inventory in sync

**4\. Hide variants from international markets**

Shopify can now hide individual variants from specific markets.

**Why not:**

- It can only say a variant exists or does not. It cannot say "only 2 of these are available to you"  
- If all variants are disabled from a market, the product returns a 404 page  
- You have 37 markets, so this means writing changes across all of them  
- The variant simply disappears with no explanation, rather than showing as unavailable

## **Problem 2: The last unit selling online** {#problem-2:-the-last-unit-selling-online}

A variant sells online when the only one left is sitting in a store, and the count turns out to be wrong.

The request is when there is only a quantity of 1 of a variant across all the locations, that variant comes off the website so the store keeps its last piece.

### **Our recommendation, if holding back 4 pieces is acceptable** {#our-recommendation,-if-holding-back-4-pieces-is-acceptable}

**Use Shopify's own safety stock setting to hold one piece back at every location.**

This is the cleanest option technically, but it comes down to whether you are comfortable with the stock it holds back. Four pieces of every variant may be more than you want sitting off the website. If so, option 1 below is the alternative.

- Shopify marks one piece at each location as unavailable, so the website can never sell it. Once the only stock left across all your locations is those held pieces, the variant shows as unavailable on the website.  
    
- This can be done with a bulk upload via Matrixify rather than custom development. Most of the work is preparing and checking the data rather than the upload itself:  
    
  - Exporting current inventory across every variant and location  
  - Building the upload file  
  - Running a small test batch to confirm it applies correctly  
  - Verifying the full run


- Additional user feedback workarounds are not needed. Your product feeds, reporting, back in stock emails, and any other 3rd party apps that rely on Shopify availability all keep working normally, because Shopify genuinely treats it as unavailable.

**Considerations**

- It holds 4 pieces of every variant off the website, one per location, rather than only the very last one. That is more stock held back, but it also cushions a counting error at every location rather than catching only the final piece.  
    
- We can apply it to specific locations rather than all four, for example the three stores only if you would rather CLS keep selling down to its last unit. It can also be applied to specific variants rather than the whole catalogue.  
    
- Anything that writes inventory to Shopify could overwrite the held piece. That includes Matrixify uploads, any integration syncing stock from another system, and any automations you have running. If any of them do, this becomes a repeatable process rather than a one-off setup, and would need to be reassessed with additional integration to consider.  
    
- New products would need the same setting applied, so it becomes part of the normal merchandising process.

### **Alternate options considered** {#alternate-options-considered-1}

**1\. Show it as unavailable on the website using custom logic**

Leaves your inventory untouched and instead changes what the website displays once stock gets low.

Custom Logic Needed:

| Piece | What it does |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Show it as unavailable on the website | Development and testing on the product page and product cards | Required |
| Cart & Checkout Validation Function | Blocks checkout, catching anyone who already had the page open or bookmarked | Optional |
| Write the count to each variant metafield | Makes the quantity readable by the checkout validation function | Required only if you want the cart & checkout validation |
| Add to cart error feedback | Explains why they cannot add it | Optional |

**Why not our first choice:** this makes the website say something Shopify does not agree with. See [**When implementing inventory workarounds that do not use native Shopify availability**](#when-implementing-inventory-workarounds-that-do-not-use-native-shopify-availability:) at the end.

**2\. A custom inventory management app**

This is the only way to get your exact rule, holding back the last piece once the three stores have 1 between them, rather than a fixed buffer at each location.

**Why not:**

- It would move your real inventory around automatically, which needs careful handling and a full activity log so you can see exactly what it did  
- It has to account for everything else that changes stock: sales, returns, transfers, imports, manual edits  
- It requires its own hosting at a monthly cost  
- We would scope it as a separate project and only recommend it if the simpler options prove insufficient

**3\. Hide or unpublish the product when stock gets low**

Using Shopify Flow to remove the product from the website and put it back when stock returns.

**Why not:**

- It does not reliably stop the sale. Anyone already holding the item in their cart can still check out  
- Unpublishing removes the whole product, every variant, not just the one that ran low  
- Unpublished products return a 404 page, and a product selling and restocking around the threshold flips repeatedly. That churn affects your Google and Meta listings and your search rankings

## **Considerations** {#considerations}

**The best solutions are the ones where Shopify itself knows an item is unavailable.** When that is true, everything downstream stays correct without extra work: your website, your product feeds, reporting, back in stock, and other integrated apps that rely on Shopify inventory data.

### **When implementing inventory workarounds that do not use native Shopify availability:** {#when-implementing-inventory-workarounds-that-do-not-use-native-shopify-availability:}

- The website says unavailable while Shopify still counts the item as in stock  
- Your product feed disagrees with the page. For example, Google and Meta compare the two and can flag a product or drop it from the feed. Some of this can be corrected, some cannot, and it depends on the platform  
- Back in stock emails will not send, because Klaviyo keys off Shopify availability and does not think the item went out of stock  
- Any other third party app that relies on Shopify availability may still treat the item as in stock  
- Introduces more custom logic, and it adds pieces that have to stay in sync and maintained

This applies to any option that shows something on the website without changing what Shopify holds.
